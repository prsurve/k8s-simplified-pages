COHDI

CoHDI (Composable Disaggregated Infrastructure) is all about breaking Kubernetes into independent, swappable layers. Today, your cluster is tightly coupled: control plane, etcd, workload nodes, networking, and storage all live together. If you want to change one piece, you often rebuild the whole thing. CoHDI flips that. Imagine running ONE shared control plane that manages 10 separate workload clusters. Or swapping your CNI plugin without touching the control plane. Or using a dedicated, remote etcd cluster shared across environments. This modularity means: lower costs (reuse components), better isolation (workloads don't share infra), easier upgrades (swap one layer at a time). It's inspired by hyperscaler architecture — Google and AWS have been doing this internally for years. For beginners: focus on vanilla K8s first. But knowing CoHDI exists helps you understand where the ecosystem is headed. Think of it as K8s growing up from a monolith into microservices for infrastructure itself.
