Your app is running, but kubectl port-forward is how you reach it from your browser, no config changes needed. If you're new to Kubernetes, a Service set to ClusterIP only answers traffic inside the cluster. Your laptop is outside, so the browser hits a wall. One command opens a tunnel from localhost:8080 straight to the Service, and you can test in seconds. The catch: the tunnel lives only as long as your terminal. Close it and the page dies. What's the first Service you ever had to debug? Tell me below. — @k8s.simplified · Kubernetes, one lesson at a time. "Your pod is running. Your Service exists. And your browser still says this site can't be reached. Here's why. A Service of type ClusterIP only answers traffic from inside the cluster. Your laptop is outside. You could edit the Service to NodePort or LoadBalancer. But to check your app right now, you don't need to touch the config at all. Run kubectl port-forward, then the service name, then your laptop port 8080, then the service port 80. Now your browser sends requests to localhost port 8080. kubectl carries each one through the tunnel and hands it to a pod behind the Service. Now close that terminal. The tunnel dies with it, and the page you were looking at stops loading. When the tunnel is live, kubectl prints a line saying it is forwarding from 127.0.0.1 port 8080 to port 80. That line is your proof it's working. Port-forward is a tunnel, not a deployment. It lives only as long as your terminal does. Comment TUNNEL and I'll send you the cheat sheet.
