Your real platform might be a ticket queue, and it's growing every day.

Your platform team's real product might be a ticket queue. Platform engineering maturity is not about having the most tools. Most teams stop at a toolchain, where Terraform and Jenkins still wait on a human to press the button. Real maturity means a developer can get a Kubernetes namespace with guardrails by filling in a small request file, no ticket required. The takeaway: a platform is mature when developers can ship without asking you. If they still need you for every step, you have a toolchain, not a platform. What's the slowest ticket your team has waited on? Drop it in the comments and I'll tell you what it's hiding. — @k8s.simplified · Kubernetes, one lesson at a time. "A developer needs a database for a new service. They open a ticket. Day one, nothing. Day four, still waiting. Most teams stop at a toolchain. Terraform, Jenkins, and a wiki page. The tools exist, but a human still presses every button. Platform maturity is about who can press the button. Turn the wiki into a template, then turn the template into a form. Inside the cluster, each team gets a namespace with a resource quota already set. Check one yourself. Now the developer submits a small request file. A pipeline creates the namespace, the quota, and the deployment in about four minutes. The ticket queue drops to zero. The catch: one team skipped the form and ran kubectl by hand. No quota. Their pod ate the node's memory, and the pod got killed. Nobody was malicious. The paved road was just slower than the shortcut, so they took the shortcut.
