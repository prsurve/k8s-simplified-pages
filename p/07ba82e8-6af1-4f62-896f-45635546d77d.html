"Your Kubernetes cluster can cost six times more, and not one line of your code changed. Every Kubernetes version has two windows. Standard support comes first, then extended support, and extended support is where the bill jumps. Step one, ask your cluster what version it runs. Run kubectl version, and read the Server line, not the Client line. Step two, ask AWS. This command gives you the version and the support type, which tells you whether the cluster is on standard or extended. Here is the mechanism. When standard support ends, the cluster moves into extended support, and the control plane goes from 10 cents an hour to 60 cents an hour. Here is the trap. Running one cluster on an extended version costs roughly 365 dollars more every month, and nothing alerts you. It just gets quietly more expensive. Your fix is to upgrade before the date. Check for removed Kubernetes features, bump your add-ons, and then move one step at a time. The rule: know your version, know its end of standard support date, and put that date on your calendar before the bill does."
