A readiness probe on the wrong port silently killed production traffic

I broke production with a readiness probe on the wrong port. Pods were running. Service was up. But zero traffic made it through because the probe was checking port 8080 while the app ran on 3000. Kubernetes marked every pod 'not ready' and pulled them from the endpoint list. It passed locally because Docker Desktop is forgiving. K8s is not. Want my 5-point readiness probe checklist? Drop 'PROBES' below and I'll send it. — @k8s.simplified · Real failures, real fixes "I shipped a deployment with a readiness probe on port 8080. Passed all local tests. Merged, deployed to prod. Ten minutes later, PagerDuty fires. Zero traffic reaching the app. Pods running. Service active. What? I describe the pod. Readiness check failing. But the app was listening on 8080... or was it? Checked the Dockerfile. App actually runs on port 3000. I copied the probe config from another service without changing the port. Why did it work locally? Docker Desktop port-forwarded everything. Kubernetes is stricter—wrong port means not ready, not ready means no traffic. Fixed the probe to port 3000, redeployed. Traffic back in 30 seconds. The probe passed, endpoints registered, service routed. Lesson: always match your probe port to the actual container port. And test rollouts in staging with real traffic, not just pod status. Now I have a checklist. Port, path, initial delay. Small config, huge blast radius. Drop PROBES if you want my 5-point readiness checklist."

PROBES

Here's my 5-point readiness probe checklist after that incident: 1. Port match: readinessProbe.httpGet.port must equal containers[].ports[].containerPort in your deployment spec. 2. Path exists: the /health or /ready endpoint you're probing actually needs to be implemented in your app and return 200 when ready. 3. initialDelaySeconds: give your app time to boot. I use 10-15s for Node apps, 20-30s for Java. If the probe fires before the app starts, it'll never become ready. 4. periodSeconds and failureThreshold: default is 10s period, 3 failures. That's 30 seconds of downtime before K8s notices. Tune this based on how fast you want detection vs how noisy your app startup is. 5. Test the rollout: deploy to staging and watch 'kubectl get endpoints <service-name>' during rollout. If endpoints drop to zero, your probe config is wrong. Fix it before prod. Bonus: always have a liveness probe too, but make it less strict than readiness. Liveness kills pods, readiness just stops traffic. You want traffic to stop before you kill the pod.
